# Residential fire and smoke safety source notes

## Scope of this slice

This slice identifies the physical objects needed to draw the fire-safety construction most directly exposed by NCC Volume Two Part H3 and Housing Provisions Section 9. It contains 115 assemblies and components across:

- fire-resisting external walls near fire source features;
- separating walls between Class 1 buildings and relevant Class 10a buildings;
- separating floors, protected horizontal projections and garage-top dwellings;
- roof-space and wall-to-roof fire-separation details;
- protected windows and doors in fire-resisting external walls;
- service penetrations, control joints and passive firestop products;
- lightweight fire-resistant wall and ceiling layers, joints and fixings;
- supporting structural fire-protection methods; and
- domestic smoke and heat alarms, interconnection and Class 1b evacuation-lighting parts.

It does not yet attempt bushfire construction under AS 3959, fire hydrants, hose reels, sprinklers, commercial detection and occupant-warning systems, smoke-control systems, fire control rooms, fire brigade facilities, fire extinguishers, every fire-door type, every cavity-barrier system or fire engineering. Those need separate regulatory and product passes.

## Regulatory path in plain English

NCC Volume Two Part H3 provides the fire-safety performance requirements and routes the house-scale Deemed-to-Satisfy construction into Housing Provisions Section 9:

- Part 9.2 covers fire separation of external walls and relevant nearby Class 10a construction;
- Part 9.3 covers separating walls and floors, their roof-space continuation, services and horizontal projections;
- Part 9.4 covers garage-top dwellings; and
- Part 9.5 covers smoke alarms and Class 1b evacuation lighting.

State and territory variations matter. South Australia, for example, adds provisions associated with brush fences. New South Wales changes parts of the garage-top and alarm paths and adds heat-alarm and durable-notice requirements in specified arrangements. A future tool must select jurisdiction, NCC edition and building classification before applying any distance, wall, floor or alarm rule.

The current national research baseline retains the exact standard editions called up by NCC 2022 Amendment 2. Standards Australia now lists AS 1670.1:2024 and AS 3786:2023 as current publications, but the Housing Provisions references remain AS 1670.1:2018 incorporating amendment 1 and AS 3786:2014 incorporating amendments 1 and 2. Newer publications are recorded as supporting metadata, not silent replacements.

## Distances and ratings are not physical objects

The Housing Provisions use distances to an allotment boundary, another building and other fire source features to decide which portion of an external wall and which openings need protection. Those distances create spatial rule zones. They are not walls, setbacks or firestop products.

A drawing tool should keep separate:

- the site boundary, nearby-building and wall geometry used to measure the trigger;
- the resulting portion of wall requiring a fire-resisting construction path;
- the physical wall, roof and opening assemblies selected to satisfy that path; and
- the evidence applying to the exact assembly and direction of exposure.

The same applies to a fire-resistance level. An FRL is performance metadata in the order structural adequacy, integrity and insulation. It is not a drawable layer. One board, block, coating or piece of wired glass does not inherit the whole assembly result.

Non-combustible and fire-resistant also mean different things. Non-combustibility classifies a material under the applicable test. Fire resistance describes how an element or assembly performs under a fire-resistance test. A wall made from non-combustible products does not automatically achieve an FRL, and an evidence-backed lightweight fire-resistant wall can contain combustible components.

## Fire-resisting external walls

A fire-resisting external wall is the complete wall over the rule-triggered extent. It can follow a masonry, masonry-veneer or tested assembly path as applicable. The model retains its structural wall, external and internal layers, base, full upper termination, openings, joints, vents, services and weatherproofing interfaces.

The wall-to-roof detail is a real assembly. Depending on the selected path, the wall continues to the roof or a protected eaves line and uses non-combustible roof, eaves, fascia, gutter or flashing construction to prevent combustible material being exposed around its top. Sarking and flashing still need to drain; a fire detail must not create a water trap.

Openings are separate protected products. The Housing Provisions include a non-openable fire window or other suitable fire-resistant construction, and a self-closing solid-core door alternative for specified external-wall openings. The catalogue keeps those separate from a tested fire-resistant doorset under AS 1905.1.

A non-openable fire window includes the fixed frame, fire-resistant pane, beads or channels, setting and retention parts, glazing seals, anchors and perimeter firestop. Wired glass alone is not a fire window. Visible wire also does not prove current impact-safety classification.

A self-closing solid-core door includes its leaf, frame, hinges, closer, latch, clearances and wall perimeter. A solid core does not turn an ordinary door into a certified fire door. Conversely, a tested fire-resistant doorset is a traceable product system whose leaf, frame, seals, hardware, tag and installation evidence remain together.

## Separating walls and roof-space continuation

A separating wall follows the boundary between the classified buildings or spaces from its required base to its required roof termination. Party wall and intertenancy wall are useful search terms, but they also appear in acoustic, title and higher-building-class contexts. The tool must know what the wall separates.

A masonry separating wall uses the existing masonry ontology for its blocks or bricks, courses, mortar, reinforcement, movement joints, openings and support. The fire-safety object records the complete wall's separating role and junction path.

A lightweight separating wall is a tested system. It can contain:

- one or more rows of steel or timber framing;
- base and head tracks;
- shaftliner panels or fire-resistant plasterboard layers;
- wall cavities and mineral-wool insulation;
- proprietary clips and deflection-head parts;
- layer-specific screws;
- staggered sheet joints;
- joint tape and compatible compound; and
- continuous fire-and-acoustic perimeter seals.

These are not a menu of interchangeable parts. The exact stud profile and spacing, lining product and layer count, joint arrangement, insulation, fasteners, perimeters and loading condition belong to one tested or assessed system. Non-uniformly scaling a component, deleting a board layer or moving a joint can break the evidence chain.

At the roof, mineral-fibre or another suitable fire-resisting material closes the gap between the wall top and roof covering where the selected detail requires it. A common eave, verandah or similar space open to more than one dwelling's roof space uses a non-combustible vertical lining and its supporting frame, joints and perimeter closure. This plane is more precise than the generic phrase fire barrier.

Where the separating wall meets a masonry-veneer external wall, a local mineral-fibre closure connects the separating plane to the veneer while preserving flashings, membranes, weep openings and cavity drainage. Filling the whole veneer cavity or blocking the flashing is not the same detail.

## Floors, projections and garage-top dwellings

A fire-resisting floor is the entire loadbearing floor and protected underside. Depending on the path, performance may come from a tested floor assembly, a protected ceiling or soffit, or a fire-protective covering beneath combustible or metal floors and incorporated beams.

A horizontal projection at a separating wall has its own protected underside extent and wall continuation. Services through that area still need to preserve the selected construction performance.

Garage-top dwelling arrangements combine protected walls, a separating floor, ceiling or soffit, supporting elements and service openings. They also have unusually important jurisdictional differences. The data therefore stores associated versus non-associated garage relationships and the direction of exposure rather than calling every plasterboard-lined garage ceiling fire rated.

## Anatomy of a passive firestop

A passive firestop is a tested or assessed system, not a red bead of caulk. Its variables include:

- the host wall, floor, ceiling or covering and its thickness;
- opening shape, dimensions and edge condition;
- every service material, size and configuration;
- annular gaps and service-to-service spacing;
- wall or floor orientation and fire-exposure direction;
- firestop products, depths, layers, backing, overlaps and fixings;
- pipe, cable tray and duct supports near the opening;
- required and achieved fire performance; and
- system certificate, installer, label and inspection record.

The catalogue separates the opening from the finished firestop. It also distinguishes pipe, cable and mixed-service penetration assemblies because an evidence-backed single-pipe detail cannot be copied around a cable bundle or combined with another detail to create an untested mixed opening.

Combustible pipe systems can use surface-mounted retrofit collars, cast-in collars or recessed intumescent wraps. A collar contains reactive material and restrains it so it closes the void left as compatible plastic softens. Pipe material, outside diameter, wall thickness, insulation, host, location and orientation all matter.

Metal pipes with combustible insulation can use another wrap or bandage arrangement. A fire bandage is not ordinary pipe lagging, and a product intended for one service cannot be selected from appearance.

Large openings can use firestop mortar, coated mineral-fibre batts or coated boards with local seals around each service. Cable openings can use sealants, putty, plugs, pillows or cable coatings. Removable products are still controlled systems; leaving extra space for future cables is permitted only where the evidence gives a fill range and reinstatement method.

Linear control and movement joints need compatible backing, compressible fill and exposed seal. The model stores joint width, depth and movement range. Rigidly filling a movement joint may create a different failure even if the fill is non-combustible.

Electrical boxes in hollow separating walls deserve their own assembly. A Housing Provisions prescriptive detail can use local framing and mineral-fibre packing behind the box. A tested system may instead use a putty pad or fire hood. Those are alternative evidence paths, not three generic layers automatically stacked together.

Firestop supports and labels are physical too. Most firestop products do not support the pipe or cable tray. A nearby clamp, bracket or hanger keeps the service aligned and limits load on the seal. A durable label links the installation to its system, installer and inspection record so later work does not unknowingly destroy it.

## Structural fire protection

Structural fire-protection objects are included as a reusable supporting family. Board enclosures, sprayed fire-resistive material, intumescent coatings and concrete encasement have different geometry and installation logic.

A board enclosure has protective sheets, supports, corner joints, fixings and connection returns. A sprayed system follows the member surface at a documented density and thickness. An intumescent coating includes compatible substrate preparation, primer, reactive basecoat and any required environmental topcoat. The ordinary shop primer or decorative finish cannot be assumed compatible.

These systems depend on the protected member, section geometry, exposure, connections and evidence. The tool can draw a specified system and store inspection measurements; it must not calculate a universal coating thickness from the word fireproofing.

## Domestic smoke and heat alarms

A domestic smoke alarm is a self-contained warning product, not merely a sensing dot. At product-detail level it can contain:

- a removable alarm head and mounting base;
- photoelectric or ionisation sensing chamber;
- insect or contamination screen;
- integral sounder;
- power, alarm and fault indicators;
- test and hush control;
- mains connector and terminal block;
- replaceable or sealed backup battery; and
- hardwired or wireless interconnection components.

The product can be classified along more than one independent axis. A photoelectric alarm can be mains powered or battery powered. A mains alarm can use a wired or wireless interconnect. A sealed lithium battery can be the operating supply or only the mains backup. These properties must not be compressed into one word such as hardwired or ten-year alarm.

Interconnection is a system relationship. A copper interconnect conductor is a physical cable and can be drawn. A radio module is a physical part, but the radio link between devices is not a solid line item unless a schematic view deliberately represents it.

The Housing Provisions place Class 1a alarms in bedroom-related corridors or intervening areas and on other storeys. Class 1b buildings also require alarms in bedrooms and associated circulation areas. Alarm location is therefore a spatial rule zone, not a room-centre point. The ceiling shape, high points, corners, exposed joists, bulkheads, fans and air outlets affect practical placement and dead-air spaces.

A heat alarm responds to temperature rather than smoke. The NSW garage-top variation uses a heat alarm and durable notices in a specified non-associated garage arrangement. That device does not replace the smoke alarms elsewhere in the dwelling.

Class 1b evacuation lighting can be an integral light in the smoke alarm or separate corridor or hallway lighting activated by the alarm. A status LED, visual alarm strobe and evacuation light are different objects: one reports device state, one warns visually and one illuminates a route.

Visual strobes, vibration pads and remote test, locate and silence controls are included as broader residential-accessibility accessories. They are not automatically part of every minimum Housing Provisions alarm layout, but a whole-building object system needs to represent them when selected for an occupant.

## CAD implications

A useful fire-safety drawing tool should retain at least:

- jurisdiction, NCC edition, building classes and relevant occupancy relationships;
- boundaries, nearby-building geometry and measured fire-source-feature zones;
- physical wall and floor extents selected by those zones;
- each assembly's exposure direction, required result and evidence reference;
- every framing, board, cavity, insulation, fixing, joint and seal layer in tested lightweight construction;
- base, roof, eaves, veneer, projection and adjoining-wall junctions;
- protected-opening product, operation, frame, infill or leaf, hardware and perimeter;
- host, opening, service, annular gap, firestop product and support geometry for every penetration;
- linear-joint route, width, movement, backing, fill and seal;
- alarm sensing type, power, backup and interconnect as separate fields;
- required alarm location zones, dead-air exclusions and physical mounting points;
- compatible alarm network, evacuation lights and accessible warning accessories; and
- certificates, installation IDs, labels, inspection records and later alterations.

At concept level, the tool can display protected wall and floor fields, opening symbols, penetration markers and alarm zones. At construction-detail level it can expose the complete board layer stack, firestop section, mounting base and cabling. Both levels should use the same stable objects so revealing detail does not change quantities or compliance metadata.

The tool must not colour a wall red and call it compliant. The result depends on classification, geometry, selected regulatory path, exact physical assembly, interfaces, installation and evidence.
