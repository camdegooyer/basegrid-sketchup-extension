# Wet-area waterproofing source notes

## Scope of this slice

This slice identifies the physical parts needed to draw internal domestic wet areas: bathrooms, showers, baths, laundries and similar rooms where water must be contained and directed to drainage. The catalogue contains 84 assemblies, substrates, prepared surfaces, membranes, bond breakers, flashings, waterstops, drain connections, shower-edge parts, vessels, tiles, sheet finishes, joints and seals.

The central object is a **wet-area waterproofing system**, not a membrane floating by itself. A workable system starts with suitable construction, continues through prepared substrates and every junction or penetration, connects to drainage, and ends at deliberate perimeter and doorway terminations. Tiles or other exposed finishes sit within that assembly but do not conceal its identity.

This pass does not cover external balconies, podiums, planter boxes, roofs, basements, pools, spas as water-retaining structures, steam rooms, commercial kitchens or below-ground tanking. A bath or spa vessel inside a domestic wet area is included only as a host and waterproofing interface.

## Two NCC Deemed-to-Satisfy routes

NCC Volume Two Part H4 establishes the health-and-amenity requirements for wet areas in Class 1 and Class 10 buildings. Its wet-area provisions point to two Deemed-to-Satisfy routes:

1. use the complete Housing Provisions Part 10.2 route; or
2. use the nominated general, material and fall clauses from Part 10.2 together with AS 3740:2021.

The two routes must not be mixed casually detail by detail. A project record needs a selected regulatory path, the building jurisdiction, NCC edition and amendment, and any applicable state or territory variation.

Housing Provisions Part 10.2 calls up AS/NZS 4858:2004 for wet-area membrane materials. The recorded standard edition is the edition named by the NCC path, even when Standards Australia has a newer project or revision in progress. AS 3958:2023 is recorded separately as a supporting current standard for ceramic and stone tiling; it does not replace the waterproofing path.

This ontology records the objects and regulatory routing. It does not reproduce licensed standards or guess membrane thicknesses, bond-breaker dimensions, protection heights, falls or installation rules that must come from the applicable clause, authorised standard and selected proprietary system.

## Protection extents are geometry, not products

The NCC describes where construction must be waterproof and where it may be water resistant around showers, baths, basins, sinks, troughs and laundries. Those protected extents are spatial requirements. They are not automatically one physical coating over every wall and floor in the room.

A future tool should therefore keep three things separate:

- the room and fixture geometry used to calculate a required protected zone;
- the required performance of each floor, wall, junction and penetration field; and
- the actual product or assembly selected to provide that performance.

This matters most at boundaries. A shower field may be waterproof while an adjoining wall field is water resistant. A waterproof surface can be a tested membrane system, an approved sheet-floor system or another accepted construction. The words *wet-area board*, *sealed* or *tanked* do not establish the correct layer or its extent.

## Read the floor and wall as ordered layers

A generic tiled wet-area floor can contain the following physical sequence:

1. structural floor or supporting frame;
2. concrete, compressed fibre-cement sheet or another suitable floor substrate;
3. substrate fixings, sheet joints, cut-edge treatment, levelling and primer;
4. bond breakers, corner bands, penetration collars and the waterproof membrane;
5. a fall-forming screed or tile bed where required by the selected build-up;
6. tile adhesive;
7. tiles;
8. regular grout joints and selected flexible movement or perimeter joints.

The sequence varies. The Housing Provisions details distinguish an internal membrane system, where the membrane is on the tile-bed side, from an external membrane system, where it is beneath the tile bed. Here *internal* and *external* describe layer position, not whether the room is indoors. Some floors form falls in the structural substrate and need no separate fall-forming screed. One mortar layer may act as both screed and tile bed, but it is still one physical layer with two recorded roles.

A wall can use concrete, cement render, fibre-cement sheet, water-resistant plasterboard or another compatible substrate. Its sheet edges, backing, fixings, joints, cut edges, corners and fixture loads remain drawable objects or attributes. A product name alone must not supply support spacing, joint treatment or membrane compatibility.

## Membranes and their accessories

The catalogue separates liquid-applied membranes from sheet membranes. It also identifies waterproof flexible sheet flooring and formed stainless-steel surfaces because a wet area is not necessarily tiled.

Membrane primer prepares the receiving surface; it is not the membrane. Reinforcement fabric or corner band strengthens or controls the membrane at a route identified by the selected system. A bond breaker allows the membrane to move across a junction instead of being rigidly bonded to three faces. Tape, sealant fillet and backing rod are possible bond-breaker forms, but their dimensions and compatibility are system inputs.

These narrow accessories can look insignificant in a model, yet they determine whether the membrane can remain continuous across wall-floor junctions, wall corners, sheet joints and movement joints. A useful construction-detail level of detail should retain their routes even when a concept model displays only one waterproofing surface.

## Shower assemblies and edge choices

The catalogue treats shower form as several independent choices:

- enclosed or unenclosed;
- raised hob, finished stepdown or level threshold;
- preformed base or site-built in-situ tray;
- point waste or linear drain; and
- screen location, opening and perimeter waterstop arrangement.

That is why *walk-in shower* is not an object definition. A walk-in shower can still have a step, can be partly screened and may be enclosed in the regulatory sense. The tool must ask about enclosure, edge level, waterstop, screen and drainage separately.

A shower hob is a raised kerb. A shower stepdown is a change between finished levels and does not prove that the structural slab was recessed. A level-threshold shower needs its own waterstop and drainage geometry rather than simply deleting the hob. An unenclosed shower also needs the protection and waterstop arrangement associated with water escaping beyond a door or screen opening.

A preformed shower base is a manufactured vessel with an outlet, supported body and upturned perimeter lip. An in-situ shower tray is built from substrate, falls, membrane, edge details and finish. The terms *base*, *tray* and *pan* are therefore retained as search language but cannot select geometry without confirming the construction.

## Junctions, flashings and terminations

The waterproof field must finish deliberately at its edges. The catalogue includes perimeter flashing, internal and external vertical flashing, shower-edge waterstop angle, doorway termination, bath-lip waterstop and an inverted channel associated with some shower-screen details.

A waterstop angle and a tile edge trim may look like the same small extrusion. The waterstop connects to or terminates the waterproofing; the tile trim protects and finishes an exposed tile edge. If one proprietary profile performs both jobs, the model can link two roles to one product instance without deleting either role.

Door jambs and architraves are not drawn first and then allowed to bury the waterproofing termination. The doorway waterstop, membrane return, jamb clearance, lining edge, tile trim, sealant and finished threshold must be coordinated as one interface.

At a bath or spa, the vessel's physical upturn, wall recess or support shelf controls how the wall protection and seal connect. A bead of visible silicone is not a substitute for the concealed bath-lip waterstop or wall waterproofing required by the chosen detail.

## Drains and penetrations

Waterproofing and plumbing meet at the membrane-drainage connection. The catalogue separates the visible grate, drain body, drainage flange or puddle flange, riser and surrounding membrane. It also separates a point floor waste from a linear drain and its channel.

The exact flange name does not prove its section. Proprietary fittings can bond, clamp or receive the membrane at different heights, and some allow moisture within a tile bed to reach the outlet. The drawing tool needs the actual fitting profile and selected membrane-system detail before it generates the drain recess, screed falls or tile cuts.

Pipe penetrations, tap bodies, mixers, shower outlets and fasteners interrupt protected surfaces. A penetration may use a compatible flexible seal, a proprietary flange or collar, or another documented detail. The ontology therefore includes penetration seals, flanges, collars and fastener seals as physical components. Simply drawing a circular hole through a membrane is incomplete.

## Tiles and sheet finishes

The tiled finish has its own physical system: wall or floor tiles, mosaic sheets, adhesive, grout, flexible sealant joints, movement-joint assemblies and edge trims. AS 3958:2023 metadata supports this family, while product compatibility still comes from the selected tile, adhesive, membrane and substrate system.

Tiles and ordinary grout are not treated as the primary waterproof layer. Grout fills regular joints. Flexible sanitary sealant replaces rigid grout at selected changes of plane, perimeters, fixtures and movement locations. A movement joint may need a clear break through the finish and bedding aligned with movement in the substrate and waterproofing detail.

Large floor tiles create a geometric constraint around point wastes because flat tiles cannot follow several intersecting fall planes without cuts or lipping. Mosaics or a linear drain can simplify that geometry, but the tool should expose the clash rather than silently warp tiles.

Non-tile finish objects include thermosetting laminate, predecorated fibre-cement sheet, sanitary acrylic lining, water-resistant flexible wall and floor sheets, and stainless-steel wet-area surfaces. Their joints, welded seams, upturns, trims, adhesive or fixing systems, penetrations and drainage connections remain essential parts of the selected assembly.

## CAD implications

A useful wet-area tool should retain at least:

- room, shower, bath, basin, sink, trough and laundry-fixture zones;
- selected NCC route and the required waterproof or water-resistant extent by surface;
- host framing or structure and every floor and wall substrate;
- sheet layout, edges, joints, fixings, cut-edge treatment, levelling and primer;
- membrane product, thickness evidence, field boundaries and layer position;
- bond-breaker, reinforcement, corner, penetration and flashing routes;
- structural, screed, membrane, drain, tile and finished-surface levels;
- fall planes and high and low points rather than one nominal slope value;
- shower enclosure, hob, stepdown, threshold, waterstop and screen arrangement;
- drain, flange, riser, grate and membrane connection profiles;
- bath, shower-base, niche, pipe, tap and fastener interfaces;
- tile or sheet-finish layout, adhesive, grout, flexible and movement joints; and
- product evidence, compatibility, installer and inspection records where the project workflow needs them.

At concept level, the tool may display a coloured protected envelope plus overall finish planes. At construction-detail level, it can expose separate substrate, membrane, screed, adhesive, finish, flange and junction objects. Both views should use the same stable object IDs so schedules and quantities do not change when detail is revealed.

The tool must not present a coloured membrane surface as proof of compliance. Compliance depends on the correct regulatory route, protection extents, products, preparation, continuity, drainage, junctions, installation and evidence.
