## Objects

- **bearer** (`AU-TF-BEARER`) — A horizontal primary floor member supported by posts, stumps, piers, walls or foundations and carrying floor joists above.
- **bottom plate** (`AU-TF-BOTTOM-PLATE`) — The lowest horizontal timber member of a wall frame, fixed to the floor or supporting structure beneath it.
- **ceiling joist** (`AU-TF-CEILING-JOIST`) — A horizontal roof-space member that supports the ceiling and, in a coupled roof, ties the lower ends of opposing rafters.
- **collar tie** (`AU-TF-COLLAR-TIE`) — A horizontal timber member fixed between a pair of opposing rafters above the wall plates.
- **common rafter** (`AU-TF-COMMON-RAFTER`) — A repeated sloping roof member running from a wall plate or eaves support towards the ridge.
- **common stud** (`AU-TF-COMMON-STUD`) — A full-height vertical wall member placed at regular centres away from the specialised framing at openings and intersections.
- **conventional timber roof frame** (`AU-TF-CONVENTIONAL-ROOF-FRAME`) — A roof structure built on site from individual rafters, ceiling joists, beams, struts, underpurlins, ties and ridge members rather than prefabricated trusses.
- **creeper truss** (`AU-TF-CREEPER-TRUSS`) — A small or shortened truss used near a hipped roof end to complete the roof surface between larger trusses and the end framing.
- **floor joist** (`AU-TF-FLOOR-JOIST`) — A repeated horizontal floor member that spans between supports and carries flooring and floor loads.
- **floor joist solid blocking** (`AU-TF-SOLID-BLOCKING`) — Short solid pieces fitted tightly between adjacent floor joists to restrain them and help distribute local effects.
- **hanging beam** (`AU-TF-HANGING-BEAM`) — A roof-space beam placed above and generally at right angles to ceiling joists so the joists can be suspended from it and have a shorter effective span.
- **head trimmer** (`AU-TF-HEAD-TRIMMER`) — A horizontal framing member forming the top edge of a non-loadbearing wall opening where a structural lintel is not performing that role.
- **hip rafter** (`AU-TF-HIP-RAFTER`) — A diagonal sloping rafter forming the external intersection of two roof planes and receiving shortened rafters from both sides.
- **hip truss** (`AU-TF-HIP-TRUSS`) — A truss used in a prefabricated hip-roof set to form or support the sloping hip portion of the roof.
- **jack rafter** (`AU-TF-JACK-RAFTER`) — A shortened sloping rafter that runs between a wall plate and a hip rafter, or between a ridge and a valley rafter.
- **jack stud** (`AU-TF-JACK-STUD`) — A shortened vertical stud installed above or below a wall opening between a plate and an opening trimmer or lintel.
- **jack truss** (`AU-TF-JACK-TRUSS`) — A shortened roof truss in a hip-roof set that is supported at one end by a girder truss or associated hip framing.
- **jamb stud** (`AU-TF-JAMB-STUD`) — A full-height vertical stud forming one side of a framed door or window opening.
- **lintel** (`AU-TF-LINTEL`) — A horizontal structural member spanning over a door, window or other wall opening.
- **nailplated timber roof truss** (`AU-TF-NAILPLATED-ROOF-TRUSS`) — A prefabricated triangulated timber roof unit whose chords and webs are joined at nodes, commonly with pressed metal nailplates.
- **nogging** (`AU-TF-NOGGING`) — A short horizontal timber member fitted between adjacent wall studs, normally in one or more rows.
- **ridgeboard** (`AU-TF-RIDGEBOARD`) — A longitudinal board at the roof ridge used to locate, align and connect the upper ends of opposing rafters.
- **roof batten** (`AU-TF-ROOF-BATTEN`) — A small repeated member fixed across rafters or truss top chords to support and secure the roof covering.
- **roof strut** (`AU-TF-ROOF-STRUT`) — A compression member installed between an underpurlin and a strutting beam or loadbearing wall to carry roof loads downward.
- **roof truss brace** (`AU-TF-TRUSS-BRACE`) — A permanent diagonal or longitudinal member fixed across a run of roof trusses to stabilise them as a group and transfer restraint forces.
- **sill trimmer** (`AU-TF-SILL-TRIMMER`) — A horizontal member forming the bottom edge of a framed window opening between its jamb studs.
- **strutting beam** (`AU-TF-STRUTTING-BEAM`) — A roof-space beam designed to receive roof struts and transfer their concentrated loads to loadbearing supports without loading the ceiling joists below.
- **timber floor frame** (`AU-TF-FLOOR-FRAME`) — The supporting timber skeleton below a floor surface, commonly including bearers, joists, trimming members and blocking.
- **timber wall frame** (`AU-TF-WALL-FRAME`) — The structural timber skeleton of a wall, assembled from plates, studs, opening members, noggings and any required bracing.
- **top plate** (`AU-TF-TOP-PLATE`) — A horizontal timber member fixed along the top of a wall frame, including across framed openings.
- **trimming joist** (`AU-TF-TRIMMING-JOIST`) — A joist beside a floor opening that receives the ends of one or more cut joists or trimmers and carries their loads to supports.
- **truncated girder truss** (`AU-TF-TRUNCATED-GIRDER-TRUSS`) — A shortened flat-topped girder truss in a hip-roof truss system that carries loads from supported hip and jack trusses.
- **truss bottom chord** (`AU-TF-TRUSS-BOTTOM-CHORD`) — The lower outer member of a roof truss, commonly horizontal, connecting the truss heels and supporting ceiling loads where designed.
- **truss nailplate** (`AU-TF-NAILPLATE`) — A toothed sheet-steel connector pressed into the faces of timber members at a truss node to join them together.
- **truss top chord** (`AU-TF-TRUSS-TOP-CHORD`) — A sloping outer member forming the top edge of a roof truss and carrying roof battens and roof loads into the truss nodes.
- **truss web** (`AU-TF-TRUSS-WEB`) — An internal timber member connecting truss chords at nodes and dividing the truss into stable triangular panels.
- **truss web tie** (`AU-TF-TRUSS-WEB-TIE`) — A longitudinal timber restraint fixed across corresponding web members in a run of roof trusses.
- **underpurlin** (`AU-TF-UNDERPURLIN`) — A horizontal or near-horizontal roof member placed under and across rafters to give them intermediate support.
- **wall brace** (`AU-TF-WALL-BRACE`) — A diagonal strap, timber member or structural sheet arrangement fixed to a wall frame to resist sideways forces.
