## Objects

- **box gutter** (`AU-RD-BOX-GUTTER`) — A generally rectangular, internally located gutter with a defined sole, sides, depth and fall, forming the main channel of a box gutter system.
- **box gutter sump** (`AU-RD-BOX-GUTTER-SUMP`) — A depressed collection box at the discharge end or low point of a box gutter, providing a larger hydraulic transition to the outlet and normally coordinating with an overflow path.
- **box gutter system** (`AU-RD-BOX-GUTTER-SYSTEM`) — An internal or concealed roof drainage assembly in which a box-section gutter collects water between or beside roof areas and discharges through a sized outlet or sump with an independent overflow path.
- **controlled gutter back gap** (`AU-RD-CONTROLLED-BACK-GAP`) — A continuous designed gap between the back of an eaves gutter and the fascia or building face, kept open by compatible spacers, clips or brackets so excess water can escape behind and below the gutter without entering the building.
- **controlled gutter front bead** (`AU-RD-CONTROLLED-FRONT-BEAD`) — A deliberately lowered or shaped outer gutter edge that establishes a continuous front overflow crest below the level at which water could flow into the building.
- **downpipe** (`AU-RD-DOWNPIPE`) — A primarily vertical pipe that carries roof water from a gutter outlet, rainhead or sump to an underground stormwater drain, tank inlet, spreader or approved discharge point.
- **downpipe bend** (`AU-RD-DOWNPIPE-BEND`) — A shaped fitting that changes the direction of a downpipe run by a stated angle while maintaining a continuous internal waterway.
- **downpipe clip** (`AU-RD-DOWNPIPE-CLIP`) — A repeated strap, saddle or proprietary bracket that holds a downpipe to a wall or frame while keeping its route, clearance and joints stable.
- **downpipe joiner** (`AU-RD-DOWNPIPE-JOINER`) — A socket, sleeve or formed connector that joins two downpipe lengths while preserving alignment and a continuous waterway.
- **downpipe offset assembly** (`AU-RD-DOWNPIPE-OFFSET`) — A short dog-leg in a downpipe made from two bends and an intermediate pipe length, used to shift the downpipe axis around an eaves projection or other obstruction.
- **downpipe shoe** (`AU-RD-DOWNPIPE-SHOE`) — A curved or angled open-ended fitting at the bottom of a downpipe that turns discharge away from the wall onto a lower roof, surface drain or approved open discharge location.
- **downpipe spreader** (`AU-RD-DOWNPIPE-SPREADER`) — A horizontal distribution fitting at a downpipe outlet that divides or spreads discharge across a lower roof so the receiving roof covering and drainage system are not loaded at one small point.
- **downpipe-to-stormwater adaptor** (`AU-RD-DOWNPIPE-STORMWATER-ADAPTOR`) — A transition fitting at ground level that connects the shape and size of an above-ground downpipe to the inlet of an underground stormwater drain or tank line.
- **eaves gutter** (`AU-RD-EAVES-GUTTER`) — An external gutter fixed along the lower edge of a roof to receive runoff discharged over the eaves end of the roof covering.
- **eaves gutter overflow system** (`AU-RD-GUTTER-OVERFLOW-SYSTEM`) — The deliberate set of openings, lowered edges, spacers or dedicated devices that lets excess water escape an eaves gutter outward before it can enter or damage the building.
- **eaves gutter system** (`AU-RD-EAVES-GUTTER-SYSTEM`) — A roof-edge drainage assembly comprising an eaves gutter, supports, stop ends, corners and joins where needed, one or more outlets, and the specified continuous or dedicated overflow measures.
- **external gutter corner** (`AU-RD-GUTTER-EXTERNAL-CORNER`) — A gutter fitting that joins two eaves gutter runs around a convex plan corner while maintaining a continuous water path.
- **front-face gutter overflow slot** (`AU-RD-FRONT-FACE-OVERFLOW-SLOT`) — One of a repeated series of purpose-formed openings through the outer face of an eaves gutter, positioned to release excess water outward below the level that could threaten the building.
- **gutter back outlet** (`AU-RD-BACK-OUTLET`) — A gutter outlet passing through the rear wall of an eaves gutter into a rainhead or offset connection rather than dropping directly through the gutter sole.
- **gutter bracket** (`AU-RD-GUTTER-BRACKET`) — A repeated support fixed to fascia, rafter ends or another designed substrate to hold an eaves gutter at its required line and fall.
- **gutter end-stop overflow weir** (`AU-RD-END-STOP-WEIR`) — A lowered, notched or purpose-opened portion of a gutter stop end that forms a dedicated overflow crest discharging visibly beyond the end of the gutter.
- **gutter expansion joint** (`AU-RD-GUTTER-EXPANSION-JOINT`) — A deliberately detailed gutter joint that permits thermal movement between gutter lengths without losing the designed water path or overflow behaviour.
- **gutter front-face overflow weir** (`AU-RD-FRONT-FACE-WEIR`) — A purpose-cut or formed broad opening in the front face of an eaves gutter, with a defined lower crest that releases excess water outward as a dedicated weir.
- **gutter guard** (`AU-RD-GUTTER-GUARD`) — A perforated, mesh or formed cover installed over a gutter or roof-to-gutter gap to reduce entry of leaves and larger debris while allowing water to enter the drainage system.
- **gutter joiner** (`AU-RD-GUTTER-JOINER`) — A sleeve, union or shaped connector that aligns and seals the ends of two compatible gutter lengths while allowing the specified joint behaviour.
- **gutter outlet** (`AU-RD-GUTTER-OUTLET`) — A formed fitting through the sole, face or end of a gutter that transfers normal drainage flow into a downpipe, rainhead or other receiving component.
- **gutter spacer** (`AU-RD-GUTTER-SPACER`) — A purpose-selected block, spacer or bracket feature that holds an eaves gutter away from the fascia to create and preserve a controlled rear overflow gap.
- **gutter stop end** (`AU-RD-GUTTER-STOP-END`) — A closed fitting sealed to the end of a gutter profile to retain normal flow, sometimes shaped or lowered to act as a dedicated overflow weir.
- **internal gutter corner** (`AU-RD-GUTTER-INTERNAL-CORNER`) — A gutter fitting that joins two eaves gutter runs around a concave plan corner while maintaining a continuous water path.
- **inverted gutter overflow nozzle** (`AU-RD-INVERTED-OVERFLOW-NOZZLE`) — A dedicated outward-projecting overflow fitting installed through the gutter face with its entry or geometry arranged to set the overflow level and discharge excess water visibly.
- **rainhead** (`AU-RD-RAINHEAD`) — An external, open-topped or visibly overflowing collection box that receives water from a gutter or roof outlet and feeds a downpipe while providing separation and overflow capacity.
- **rainhead overflow opening** (`AU-RD-RAINHEAD-OVERFLOW-OPENING`) — A visible opening, lowered edge or hole in a rainhead that releases surcharge before the incoming gutter or internal drainage path can back up into the building.
- **rainwater chain** (`AU-RD-RAINWATER-CHAIN`) — An exposed chain or linked cup assembly that guides water downward from a gutter outlet to a receiving drain or basin as an open alternative to a closed downpipe in suitable locations.
- **rectangular downpipe** (`AU-RD-RECTANGULAR-DOWNPIPE`) — A downpipe with a rectangular or near-rectangular cross-section, assembled from matching pipe lengths, bends, offsets, clips and lower connection fittings.
- **roof drainage system** (`AU-RD-ROOF-DRAINAGE-SYSTEM`) — The above-ground assembly that collects water from roof catchments, carries it through gutters and outlets, provides safe overflow paths, and delivers it to a downpipe, tank inlet or underground stormwater system.
- **roof gutter** (`AU-RD-GUTTER`) — A long open channel at a roof edge or low line that intercepts runoff and conveys it to an outlet.
- **round downpipe** (`AU-RD-ROUND-DOWNPIPE`) — A downpipe with a circular cross-section, assembled from compatible round pipe lengths, sockets or joiners, bends, clips and lower connection fittings.
- **soaker gutter** (`AU-RD-SOAKER-GUTTER`) — A narrow concealed or partly concealed channel that receives water beside a roof abutment or beneath covering edges and conveys it to a lower gutter or roof surface.
- **valley gutter** (`AU-RD-VALLEY-GUTTER`) — A formed open channel running down the internal angle where two roof planes meet, receiving water from both sides and carrying it toward the eaves or another collector.
